# Orthopedics clinic website: content checklist

Every piece of text on the site gets checked against this list before it goes live. That covers page copy, headings, buttons, form messages, meta descriptions, alt text, emails and SMS.

The test is simple. Would a doctor or receptionist at this clinic say this sentence to a patient across the desk? If not, rewrite it.

## 1. The text sounds like a person wrote it

- [ ] Short, plain sentences. A patient with knee pain should understand them on the first read.
- [ ] Sentence lengths vary. A few short ones, then a longer one.
- [ ] The page talks to the patient as "you" and the clinic speaks as "we"
- [ ] Contractions are used: we're, you'll, don't
- [ ] Medical terms are explained the first time they appear
- [ ] Real details replace adjectives: years of practice, number of surgeries, actual opening hours
- [ ] Every number, qualification and claim is true and comes from the clinic
- [ ] No invented patient stories, reviews or statistics
- [ ] Each page opens with something concrete, not "Welcome to our website"
- [ ] The page stops when the point is made. No summary paragraph at the end.

## 2. Words and phrases to remove

- [ ] No brochure words: world-class, state-of-the-art, cutting-edge, renowned, seamless, holistic, commitment to excellence
- [ ] No filler verbs: leverage, utilize, delve, foster, elevate, empower, streamline, unlock, showcase
- [ ] No "journey" for treatment or recovery
- [ ] No "It's not just X, it's Y" sentences
- [ ] No openers like "In today's fast-paced world" or "Have you ever wondered"
- [ ] No sentences starting with Moreover, Furthermore, Additionally
- [ ] No "It's important to note" or "It's worth mentioning"
- [ ] No "In conclusion", "Overall" or "To sum up"
- [ ] No vague claims like "experts say" or "studies show" without a named source
- [ ] No groups of three adjectives used out of habit
- [ ] No leftover placeholders such as [City], [Doctor name] or "Lorem ipsum"

## 3. Formatting

- [ ] No em dashes or en dashes. Use a comma, a full stop or "to".
- [ ] Headings in sentence case, not Title Case
- [ ] No bold scattered through paragraphs
- [ ] Lists only for real steps or items a patient will scan
- [ ] One exclamation mark per page at most
- [ ] Straight quotes and apostrophes throughout

## 4. Emoji

- [ ] No emoji anywhere on the website: headings, body text, buttons, menus, form messages, meta titles and descriptions
- [ ] Where a symbol helps (phone, location, clock, email), use an SVG icon in the theme colour, all from one icon set
- [ ] No emoji used as bullet points or in front of headings, anywhere
- [ ] In social posts, WhatsApp or SMS, where an emoji may fit, use at most one, placed at the end of a sentence the way a person would type it
- [ ] Pick a plain one a person would send: a simple smile, folded hands, a thumbs up
- [ ] Never use the ones that mark text as machine-written: sparkles, rocket, check mark, pointing finger, light bulb, target, fire, flexed arm, star
- [ ] If the message reads fine without the emoji, leave it out

## 5. Medical content

- [ ] A doctor at the clinic has read and approved every medical page
- [ ] The reviewer's name and the review date appear on the page
- [ ] No promise of a cure or a guaranteed result
- [ ] Each condition page says when to see a doctor
- [ ] The medical disclaimer is linked from the footer

## 6. Final read

- [ ] Read the page aloud once. Fix any sentence that sounds stiff.
- [ ] Spelling and grammar checked
- [ ] Doctor names, degrees, phone numbers and hours checked against the clinic's own records
- [ ] Nepali text, when added, is written by a Nepali speaker and not machine-translated
